## Implementation notes (v2)

Live: https://springyworks.github.io/eftelaing-dust/ (built with `npm run build`, copied to `docs/index.html`).

- **Dust enters at L10** (the Director) only. It reaches the lower layers solely through the top-down coupling slider.
- **Maslow x OSI+ matrix** (maxGraph): Maslow levels are rows, OSI+ layers are columns. Views: activity (OSI+ to Maslow), unmet need (Maslow to OSI+) and the editable mapping weights. Click a cell to cycle its weight (0, 0.5, 1), a column header to strike a layer, a row header to strike every layer mapped to that level. The "Maslow to OSI+ drive" slider lets unmet needs excite quiet layers (reverse mapping).
- **Wave-tank monitor** (replaces the earlier decorative instrument): every layer is an FM voice driven by measurements of its tank. Pitch = measured frequency, level = rms, FM index = peakiness (crest factor) and phase-lock with neighbours, plus spike detection (sudden velocity jumps) with LEDs, counters, a bright FM pluck and a live spectrum. Pitch is a fixed time-scaling (1600 Hz per rad/step), so it shows relative, not physical, frequencies.
- Artistic simulation, not a validated neuroscience model.

## Implementation notes (v3)

- **Monte Carlo auto-exerciser** (`src/exerciser.ts`, `src/taste.ts`): it glides the sliders to random proposals, lets you listen, then pops up a dialog with **No / Maybe / Yes** (keys `B` `N` `M` `Y`, optional stronger `L` Hellish and `H` Heavenly). Optional "what is off?" chips (too flat / too wild dynamics, dull / harsh voices, stiff / messy rhythm) steer the next proposal by nudging the sliders they name; Boring also raises exploration. Starting it switches the sound on. A Gaussian-process model learns your taste from the ratings (Hellish=-0.5, No=0, Boring=0.25, Maybe=0.5, Yes=1, Heavenly=1.5) and proposes new settings (local moves, moves around favourites, global jumps) by predicted liking plus an exploration bonus. The taste profile shows per-slider relevance, click a row to lock a slider. "Play my favourite" jumps to the best known setting, "Forget everything" resets. Ratings persist in `localStorage`. `speed` and `volume` are never auto-moved.
- **Sand bed** (`src/sim.ts`): every tank has a sand bed that changes the local wave speed (shallow = slow). Waves erode: sand moves from energetic cells to quiet cells (Chladni / streaming), and gravity levels it out again. Transport is face-to-face flux, so **sand mass is conserved exactly**, it only redistributes. Sliders: erosion, levelling, sand depth effect. Buttons: "Level the sand", "Seed random dunes". Scenario "Sea builds a beach" shows it.
- **Projections** (`src/render.ts`): wave, sand relief, sand + water, isometric and beach profile.
- **Stereo / HRTF jazz** (`src/audio.ts`): each layer is a source placed in a binaural scene (azimuth from layer and tank centroid, elevation by layer, distance by lock). Parametric HRTF after `knowlege_source/Hearing.md`: Woodworth ITD, head-shadow shelf, pinna notches, back shelf, air absorption, synthetic room reverb. Alternative modes: plain stereo pan, mono. Pitches snap to a Dm11 | G13 | Cmaj9#11 | A7b9b13 loop (92 bpm) with swing. The scene card shows the cues. Use headphones.
- **Jazz band played by the whole brain** (`src/band.ts`): layer 1 (Physical) is the rhythm section: its tank drives the walking bass, ride and the comping activity. With *Brain plays the band* up, the other layers join: L4 sets the comping peakiness, L7/L8 steer the melody, L10 (Director) conducts the tempo, and every Maslow level feeds the harmony (low fulfilment = tense Cmaj9#11 / A7b9b13, high = resolved Cmaj9 / A13). Brain health (alignment + stability) decides how tight and in tune it plays; Dust loosens the timing; spikes and FIRE add accents. The **layer choir** gives L2 to L10 one MIDI voice each (chord tones from the pitch the tank measures, louder when it spikes). Every voice, the band's four instruments and the ten choir voices, is its own binaural source with the HRTF cues of `knowlege_source/Hearing.md` (ITD, head shadow, pinna notch, room), at its place in the scene card.
- **Where the notes come from (no dice)**: the tune is a theme read off the layers. Theme A is a scan line through layer 1's sand bed (the terrain the waves carved) plus its live wave, eight samples = the eight eighth-notes of a bar, turned into scale steps on the current chord; theme B is read the same way from the loudest upper layer. Form over the eight-bar loop: A A A A | B B | A A, and every odd bar answers with a fresh live reading in its tail, so the music recurs but keeps developing as sand and waves change. The **rhythm is the synchroniser**: slot k of the bar belongs to the phase lock between layers k+1 and k+2 and speaks when that pair is more in sync than average (also for extra comping stabs), its loudness follows that layer's activity. All former `Math.random()` decisions now read the wave fields of the tanks. The cues panel shows the current theme, its source line, its steps and the sync pattern (x = speaks).
- **You steer, it obeys, then forgets**: while Monte Carlo runs, any slider you move is obeyed at once and held where you put it; your move fades with a half-life set by *Respect my moves (s)* (default 40 s), so the exerciser slowly regains freedom and creativity is not locked out. Gripped sliders show in orange in the taste profile, the status line says how many you are steering, and the listen timer waits while you tune. What you set is also what gets rated, so it teaches the model.
- **The brain learns to play**: bass, keys, lead, ride, choir level and brain share are ordinary sliders, so the Monte Carlo exerciser explores them and learns from your No / Maybe / Yes / Heavenly answers what band you like.
- **General audio settings** (collapsed group in the sidebar): *Audio mode* has three listening options: **1. Raw FM synthesiser** (the original nervous audification of all ten layers, for inspecting the system), **2. Internal music** (the whole brain plays the MIDI band), **3. Layer 1 alone plays the synthesiser**; plus Raw + music together. Sliders: raw FM level, band level, band busyness, brain share, choir level, bass / keys / lead / ride levels and tempo (explored by the Monte Carlo exerciser, except the two master levels and volume, which you own). *MIDI output* optionally sends the band to the first Web MIDI port (bass ch 1, keys ch 2, lead ch 3, choir ch 4-9 and 11-13, drums ch 10).
- **Automatic level control, no clipping** (`src/audio.ts`): the master output goes through an ALC gain, a fast limiter (20:1, 2 ms attack) and a tanh soft clipper, so the output cannot exceed full scale. The ALC watches the peak of everything about to be played, pulls the gain down at once when it passes the *Level ceiling (ALC)* slider (you own it, the exerciser never moves it) and recovers slowly (about 1.5 s), like a homeostatic reflex. The audio scene shows `ALC -x.x dB` (and PEAK if the ceiling was hit). Voice onsets now have 2-3 ms attacks and the lead no longer steps in gain mid-note, which removes clicks.
- **Scratchable tanks**: drag a finger or mouse through any tank (the big tank in every projection, and each of the ten thumbnails) to draw ripples into that layer, as in Falstad's ripple tank; a tap on the big tank still drops one pulse and a tap on a thumbnail selects it.
- **Instruments are chosen by the layers** (`src/band.ts`): the walking bass, keys, lead, ride and every choir voice used to be fixed recipes. Now each has a patch table (bass: upright / fretless / synth / sub; keys: e-piano / vibes / organ / harp / warm pad; lead: saw / flute / muted trumpet / clarinet; ride: ride / brushes / hat; choir: bell / glass / marimba / celesta / tine). The patch is the *timbre signature* of the driving layer (where its energy sits, how peaky it is, how phase-locked), a wrapped read so small field differences reach every patch. Bass is read from L1, keys from L4, ride from L6 every 8 bars, the lead from the layer that wrote the current theme. With little brain share everything is read from L1. A MIDI program change is sent half way through a swap, and the cues panel lists the current instruments (a swap shows as `flute→saw lead 50%`).
- **Instruments change like a musician changes them**: never a hard cut. One voice swaps at a time at different bars of the eight-bar form (lead at the theme change, bass at bar 3, ride at bar 4, keys at bar 7), the signature must lie clearly inside another patch (no flicker at a border), at least a set number of bars must have passed since the last swap (slider *Instrument changes*, 0 freezes them), and the swap is a two-bar equal-power crossfade: the old instrument fades out while the new one fades in. A voice at level 0 swaps at once. The layer choir glides its FM ratio note by note instead of jumping.
- **More feedback chips, grouped** (Dynamics, Voices, Space, Rhythm, Tempo, Form, Harmony; 26 in all, shown in the panel and in a larger popup): besides flat / wild, dull / harsh, stiff / messy there are *A voice dominates* / *A voice is lost* (the band keeps a smoothed loudness per voice, so the loudest or the most buried voice is pulled down or up), *Bass too thin* / *too boomy*, *Too washy* / *too dry*, *Too mechanical* / *too sloppy*, *No groove*, *Too busy* / *too sparse*, *Drags* / *Rushed*, *Too little variation* / *No hook*, *Jarring instrument change*, *Melody lost*, *Want more layers* and *Too tense* / *Harmony too plain*. They move the band sliders and four new brain sliders the exerciser also learns: **Variation** (passing and grace notes, shifted phrases, extra answers, rhythm slots joining), **Human feel** (0 = machine-exact timing and dynamics; 1 = loose timing from the wave fields, swelling velocities, lead behind the beat), **Instrument changes** and **Harmonic colour** (biases the Maslow-driven tension: sweet and resolved to altered and tense).
- **Type into layer 0 (L1)**: the text box under the big tank strikes L1 with every character; the band plays the character as a lead note at once, and the brain *answers*: a few slots later the loudest upper layer echoes the note (choir voice) and a keys stab follows. The raw FM pluck is heard in raw/both modes, the MIDI echo in the music modes. Enter drops a stronger pulse.
- **Free-text feedback and AI relay** (`src/feedback.ts`, `server/relay.ts`): next to the Monte Carlo buttons there is a free note field (and one in the popup, sent with the rating and "what is off" chips). Notes are queued in `localStorage` with a snapshot (sliders, instruments, chord, bpm, theme, brain state), and flushed to the relay whenever it is reachable (retry every 15 s, nothing is lost while it is down). Run `npm run relay` (HTTP on 127.0.0.1:8787, also serves `dist/index.html`, only localhost and the GitHub Pages origin are allowed, notes go to the git-ignored `feedback/notes.jsonl`). Run `npm run mcp` (or use `.vscode/mcp.json`) for an MCP stdio server with the tools `list_feedback`, `ack_feedback` and `feedback_stats`, so a local or external AI assistant can read the open notes with their context, analyse which features are really lacking, and acknowledge them with a reply. The relay is bundled with esbuild (`.relay/`, git-ignored) because plain Node here cannot run `.ts`.
- **Styles, licks and a brain that learns to swing** (`src/style.ts`, `src/band.ts`): the band is no longer one tune. Five styles (swing, bebop, fusion, funk, bossa) each define a swing ratio (a real 2:1 triplet feel at full swing), tempo, bass (walking with chromatic approach and skip notes, 16th funk lines with octave pops and ghost notes, bossa root-fifth), comping (charleston with anticipations, chicken-scratch 16ths, bossa clave), a drum groove (ride/feathered kick/ghost snare; funk and fusion kick, snare and 16th hats; bossa rim clave) and 2 to 4 chord progressions each (m9, m11, dom7/9/13, alt, maj9, 8 bars). The lead plays **phrases** read off the layers instead of one theme note per slot: eighth-note swing lines and bebop runs with enclosures, 16th fusion bursts, and funk riffs that repeat for 2 to 4 bars with mutations and fills. *Band style* (Auto or locked), *Licks and riffs*, *Groove* and *Brain learns* sliders sit in the general audio settings. In Auto the brain picks a style from its layers (activity, peakiness, tension, upper-layer energy) plus what you liked, and reconsiders every 8 to 16 bars; jazz is its home style. A persistent **personality** (`localStorage`, key `eftelaing-dust.brain.v1`) grows like a virtual child: playing time, the phase lock between the layers and your ratings raise swing skill, lick vocabulary and per-style mastery (child, student, sideman, soloist, bandleader); a young brain plays looser in time. Hint chips *Not swinging*, *No riffs / licks*, *Same old tune* and *Want bebop / swing / fusion / funk / bossa* teach it directly. This is a metaphor driven by your feedback and the coherence of the tanks, not a neural network. The cues panel shows style, progression, swing %, current phrase and the personality.
- **Nice / Try again (reward, never punishment)**: two big buttons at the top. *Nice* is sugar: it raises your liking of the style that plays, the taste for every pattern sounding right now (riff rhythm, bass line, comping, kick, progression) and the brain's skills; clicks within 4 s stack (up to x6). *Try again* only makes those patterns a little less likely and makes the band curious; skill and mastery never go down. Learning is continuous: patterns are drawn with weights from this taste (`Personality.pick`), and both buttons also vote for the current slider setting in the Monte Carlo taste model.
- **Sand memory (learning without back-propagation)**: by default the learning lives in the sand beds. The coupling between layer l and l+1 at each cell is carved by the sand of both beds (`Simulation.cpl`, exponential in the dune height: dunes open the link, hollows close it), so the stack is a 3D medium (x, y, layer) whose vertical conductivity is sand. Nice injects a reward hormone (`Simulation.reward`, decays with tau 6 s); while it is positive, sand of each layer climbs the gradient of a trace of "where am I in phase with my neighbour layers" (mass-conserving face fluxes, same scheme as erosion), a negative shot moves it away and slides the beds part-way back to ~12 s ago. Normal levelling forgets slowly. A selector in the "Brain learning" card also offers per-link gains (three-factor rule) or both. Replaying the same pulse after 8 Nice shots (headless, 3 runs): sand only phase lock 0.355 to 0.427 and neighbour-layer energy +39%, link gains only 0.49 to 0.61, both together 0.355 to 0.484. The card shows the coupling map of the selected link; the beds are saved in the browser.
- **The tanks really learn (reward hormone)**: Nice also injects a shot of "dopamine" (`Simulation.reward`, decays with tau 6 s) that gates plasticity of the nine inter-layer couplings (`gain[l]`, 0.5..2x, innate = 1): a three-factor rule where links that were recently phase-locked (eligibility trace) strengthen, most strongly next to the layer you watch. Try again is a negative shot: it weakens them and rolls the wiring part-way back to ~12 s ago. Slow forgetting keeps the system open. The band reads the layer locks, so the new wiring changes the music. The "Brain learning" card shows hormone and couplings live, has a *Prove it* A/B experiment (headless run: phase lock 0.49 without reward vs 0.61 after eight Nice shots) and a *Forget learned wiring* button.
- **Full ringing chords**: on every chord change (and again halfway) the Keys play the whole chord, a left-hand root plus the 4-note voicing rolled upwards, on top of the short stabs.
- **Running riffs that develop**: the lead plays eight kinds of runs (scale run, arpeggio, chromatic run into the next chord, zigzag, four sequence cells, scale with a turn), weighted by what you rewarded with Nice / Try again, with a swelling velocity arc, enclosures and a held last note. Its direction follows layer 6 (rising or fading energy). The next bar often answers it by inversion, retrograde or a step shift, and funk riffs are developed on every repeat. Dice numbers above layer 10 wrap around the stack.
- **Evolving riff rhythms**: the pool of riff rhythms is a population. Every few bars (Evolution slider, 0 frozen) a child is mutated from a parent chosen by taste and replaces the least-liked rhythm when the pool is full; the pool and the generation are saved with the brain in `localStorage`.
- **Voice in (microphone)**: the Mic button in the big-tank card turns your voice into MIDI (`src/voice.ts`: YIN pitch, adaptive noise gate, onset detection, note on/off with staccato flag, about 46 ms latency). Sung notes strike layer 1 at a place set by pitch, are heard as instruments (optional) and the brain answers a few slots later, snapped to the chord; unpitched bursts ("pfff", "tss") become drum sticks. Use headphones.
- **Beat lattice (up-down-up)**: ten phase oscillators, one per layer, anchored at layer 1 to the band's audible grid and coupled upwards with a phase lag (`src/beat.ts`), so the beat climbs the stack in one bar and descends in the next. The link strengths come from the sand memory (`sim.learned`), and with the **Beat drive** slider each tank is gently pulsed when its phase passes the beat, so the vertical sync is real tank dynamics the sand can learn from. The 3D stack glows on the beat.
- **Human follows the machine**: a beat cue (bar dots, a ring that closes on the next beat) and a timing meter (-100..+100 ms) show how early or late your sung or clapped onsets land on the band's grid, with the output latency and the detection delay compensated. Hits within 70 ms give the brain a small reward (at most one per 1.5 s). "Machine follows my tempo" is optional and off by default.
- **4D view (x · y · layers · time)**: `src/tesseract.ts` keeps 5 s of the whole stack at 10 Hz and measures what the lattice only claims: per-layer beat sync, the vertical flow (lag between neighbouring layers, up or down), and the neighbour-layer field correlation over time, drawn as layers-by-time heat maps.
- **WebGPU solver** (`src/gpu.ts`, `Simulation.attachGpu` / `flushGpu`): all ten tanks (1D, 2D and 3D) are solved by two WGSL compute kernels on the graphics card: the step (the same maths as the CPU loop: `v += a`, `u += v`, clamp at ±2, Dirichlet walls, sand-memory coupling) and an op kernel that adds everything the CPU used to write into the fields (finger scratches, fires, the drives, dust) just before the step it belongs to. The CPU keeps a mirror of `u`/`v` that is read back one or two frames late; the sand, the observables, the band and the renderers read the mirror. Check against the CPU solver in the browser: the fields agree to about 5e-7 over 60 steps, with drives and scratches, in 1D, 2D and 3D, and the rms and stability of a live run match. The **Wave solver** select (General audio settings) switches between WebGPU and CPU; without WebGPU, or when the device is lost, the CPU solver takes over by itself, and the status line says which one runs. Open the page with `?debug` to get `window.__eftelaing` (sim, params, audio) for comparisons.
- **Voice auto-tuner** (`src/tuner.ts`, the *tune-in* slider in the microphone card): what you sing is pulled to what the band plays now. Every candidate within three semitones costs its distance plus a penalty for its class (chord tone 0, scale tone or tension 3s², chromatic passing note 6s); the strictness `s` is the tune-in level times (1 − 0.7 × harmonic colour), so the jazzier (the more tense) the band, the more relaxed the tuner: at 0 you are raw, at full strictness only chord tones survive, in between scale tones and tensions are left alone and passing notes are pulled to the nearest tone. The tuned note is what the monitor plays, what the brain answers and where the bottom tank is struck; the readout shows `you sang C3, the band hears E3 (chord tone, pulled +1 semitone)` and the held-note line shows the target (`D#3→E3`).
- **Quick message box** (`src/quickchat.ts`, top bar right of Try again): free text plus a popup of selectable keywords (praise such as "Oh nice!" and "What a transition!", the "what is off" chips, transitions, bug, idea). Known keywords steer the band at once, praise counts as a Nice, everything is relayed as a note.
- **MCP slider control** (`src/remote.ts`, `server/relay.ts`): the MCP tools `get_state` (every slider with range, style, phrase, brain) and `set_sliders` let an AI assistant move sliders of the running app (clamped, only known names, treated as your own move by the exerciser). Every note, ack and command carries a Unix time in milliseconds (`ts`, plus `receivedTs` at the relay) and notes are listed in `ts` order, so late or out-of-order arrivals stay readable. Restart the MCP server after updating the relay.
- **Tickle any layer**: a tap on any thumbnail in the layer palette now pokes that layer at the tapped spot (you hear it) and selects it; dragging scratches it, like on the big tank.
- **Snare and fills**: every snare hit differs (ghost notes are low and brushy, loud ones sometimes crack as a rimshot, medium ones get a flam, pitch and decay wobble), and at the end of every fourth bar (not bossa) the drummer may play a crescendo fill on snare, toms and kick; fluent, groovy brains fill more often.
- **Baroque style (Bach ingredients)**: a sixth style in the selector and the Auto pick. Straight time, a running eighth-note bass through the chord tones, no drums, chord progressions that follow the circle of fifths (Am-Dm-G-C, C-F-Bm7b5-E7), and a lead that spins a four-note motif (contour read from the theme) as a sequence stepping down, up in odd bars, with mordents and trills on the last note of odd bars. A **canon**: the Keys answer last bar's lead phrase an octave lower, fitted onto the current chord. Hint chip *Want Bach*.
- **3D stack** (`src/stack3d.ts`): a card with the ten tanks as plates in an orthographic canvas-2D view (no WebGL). Plate image = wave field, rim thickness = amplitude, vertical lines = link coupling (sand memory) x recent in-phase coherence, dots run from the louder to the quieter layer. Drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, tap a plate to select and tickle it.
- **Scratching plays music**: dragging through any tank also plays notes. The position along the tank picks a note of the current chord's scale, speed and height give the loudness, tank L1 plays the lead voice and every other tank its own choir voice, plus a dry sand rasp. The notes go out over MIDI too, and the brain picks up vocabulary from watching you play.
- "rfti" in the original request was read as HRTF.
- **Expression and three new keys patches** (`src/fx.ts`, `src/band.ts`): a reusable auto-wah (an audio-rate envelope follower driving a resonant bandpass filter, no two notes sound quite the same) now sits on three new instrument voices: a real Fender-Rhodes tine (bell FM, a bark on hard hits, built-in tremolo), a struck-string piano, and a clavinet (the funky Stevie-Wonder bite). The strum guitar lost its fixed filter sweep for the same touch-wah, strums are no longer struck at a metronomic, identical speed ("a one-handed pendulum"), and it gained two new strokes: **flam** (a soft grace down-strum just ahead of the main one) and **chuck** (a percussive all-string mute slap, no pitch).
- **Chord plan and strum guitar** (`src/harmony.ts`): the chords are no longer the same fixed voicing. Every chord is voice-led from the previous one (inversions chosen to move as little as possible) in one of four voicings (shell, rootless, spread, upper structure) that change every bar. A slow 32-bar arc of "heat", pushed up by the activity of **layer 6** and the Maslow tension, decides how adventurous the harmony is: tritone substitutions of dominants, m11 / #11 colours, and approach chords (the dominant, or a dominant a semitone above) for the last two beats of a chord. The cues panel shows the current chord, voicing, heat and substitution. **Layer 5** picks the comping rhythm: eight swing comping patterns and eight chicken-scratch patterns, and ten strum patterns (down, up, accent, muted chop) for the new *strum guitar* keys patch; Nice / Try again teach which ones you like. The walking bass also syncopates now: a note is pushed onto the "and" before its beat.
- **A slow build/release arc, and a real "switch this layer off" test** (`src/band.ts`, `src/sim.ts`): layers 6 and 7 (Presentation, Application) now drive a slow (tens of seconds) arrangement arc, not just the harmonic heat: while they run hotter than their own recent average the band gets denser and louder (comping density, velocity, choir voices joining, fill chance all scale with it), and it strips back to a sparse bass-and-ride texture as they settle, always drifting back towards a neutral middle so it never gets stuck high or low. The cues panel's new `arrangement` line shows it rising, falling or steady. Every layer tile also has a small **⏻** button: click it to switch that tank off (not a mix mute — its wave field is decoupled from its neighbours and damped to flat, sand untouched) and hear and watch whether the band really answers to it.
- **Bug fix: a switched-off layer, or all of them, kept playing** (`src/sim.ts`, `src/band.ts`): a real mute now blocks energy at the source — drives (instrument hum, player activity, director intent, pain/delight/curiosity/ecstasy, dust) and fire/scratch all now check the layer's mute flag before injecting anything, instead of only softening the coupling. Verified: with every layer switched off, eight seconds of play produced zero bass, comp, lead, choir, kick, snare, ride or pluck calls (all were nonzero before the fix). Also found and fixed: the choir voices were gated only by the brain-share slider, not by whether that layer actually had any measured activity, so they (and the chord changes they outlined) kept sounding through a "silent" layer. With layer 1 muted and brain share up, the rest of the band (bass through choir, over a hundred events in eight seconds) keeps playing from the other nine layers, confirming the band is not just reading layer 1.
- **"From the brain" is now the default band style — no preset patterns at all** (`src/band.ts`, `src/style.ts`): the genre tables (swing/bebop/funk/bossa patterns and progressions) still exist as selectable styles, but the default plays nothing from a table. Rhythm: bass, kick, ride, snare and keys each speak on every Mth crest of their own tank's measured oscillation (L1 bass+kick, L2 ride, L3 snare, L4 keys), each hit placed where the crest really falls — off the grid, drifting against the band as the tank's pitch drifts. The sand bed accents the hits where it has piled up (the learned terrain is the groove memory), the snare only speaks when layer 3's phase-lock to its neighbour is at or above the stack's average (the synchroniser as a drummer), and a quiet or switched-off tank says nothing. Harmony: the chord is measured from the layers' own pitches — every audible layer votes with its pitch class, the bottom three propose the root, the best-overlapping quality wins, and a new chord must win two boundaries in a row (hysteresis) so silence keeps the chord instead of resetting it. The cues panel says e.g. "E9 measured from the layers". Verified: tickling layer 3 moved the chord (Dm9 → E9 → Bb9), muting all layers gives true silence.
- **The felt life of L9 is on by default**: delight, interest, curiosity and ecstasy now default to 0.5 (pain stays 0, Director intent 0.5), so the upper layers live, the top of the Maslow pyramid sits near 50% instead of 0, and there is something up there to tickle and to hear. The two pure-physics demo scenarios (Bottom-up cascade, Director conducts) still zero them.
- **Audio health lamp: clipping and dead branches, everywhere in the chain** (`src/meter.ts`): a round glass top left that fills blue with the output level. The ring turns **red** when any stage clipped (and the fill flashes red), **orange** while the ALC is pulling more than 3 dB (the "swinging out of the clipping space" pumping), **purple** when a branch is dead. Analyser taps sit at every stage - each band voice bus (bass, keys, lead, ride), the choir, the raw FM voices, the mix going into the ALC, and the output after the safety chain - so a clip is blamed on the stage where it really happened, not guessed. A stage that is being *sent* notes but produces silence for over six seconds is flagged **DEAD** (the failure a VU bar hides). Click the lamp for the full report: per-stage peaks in dB, clip counts with age, dead/idle marks, ALC reduction, and the number of **live synthesiser sources** (every oscillator/noise/constant source is counted in and out), so a clogging audio graph is visible as a number that climbs instead of a feeling. The MCP snapshot carries the same report. Verified: a deliberate overload in the bass bus was blamed as "CLIPPING: band bass" (36 counts), a silently broken keys branch was flagged "DEAD: notes are being sent but no sound comes out" after 16 s and recovered on repair, and live sources stayed level (~44-52) during normal play - no node leak.
- **Speed test and fallback** (`src/pacing.ts`): 1.5 s after load the app measures real WebGPU steps/s (readbacks that completed) and CPU steps/s, picks the faster solver, lowers Speed if even that is too slow, and tells you in a notice bar (with *Re-test speed*). Without WebGPU the CPU solver is the backup and the notice says so. A phone warning explains that the solver runs on one core and drains the battery. If the frame rate stays under 16 fps a governor lowers Speed, then the tank dimension, and says so.
- **Share link** (`src/share.ts`): the blue *share link* at the top right copies one long URL (about 3.6 k characters, deflate + base64url in the `#s=` fragment) with all sliders and selects, the mic tuner level, the sand wiring, the band personality and your taste ratings. Opening it asks for confirmation, then replaces what that browser had learned. Links made on localhost point at the github.io demo. The *GitHub* link next to it opens the repo.
