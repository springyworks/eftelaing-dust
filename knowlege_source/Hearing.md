Academic spatial hearing theory (psychoacoustics) and synthetic spatial audio synthesis both rest on a single core premise: the human auditory system has no direct "spatial retina" on the basilar membrane. Unlike vision, where spatial coordinates map directly to photoreceptors, the cochlea only performs a 1D frequency-to-place decomposition.

Every 3D coordinate—azimuth (
θ), elevation (

A synthetic spatial synthesizer reverses this biological process: it applies mathematical filters to a dry monophonic signal to forge those exact physical cues before the sound reaches the eardrums.
